The Arizona Secretary of State (SOS) results site (results.arizona.vote, served by app.enhancedvoting.com) labels the Nov 3, 2026 general election results "Unofficial Results" (data as of Nov 19, 2026, 4:35 PM CST; pulled Nov 19, 2026). All 15 counties report fully. The statewide canvass is scheduled for Nov 23, 2026, so these pages need a re-run after it. 2022 figures are the official SOS canvass of Dec 5, 2022. P = Democratic share of ACTIVE registered voters, from the G.E. (general election) row of the SOS State Voter Registration reports (2022 General Election and November 2026). Governor race only. Votes are votes for the Democratic candidate, not a count of registered Democrats who voted. These pages measure a pattern; they make no claim about intent, fraud or cause.

The same arithmetic at three levels: one county, then the six largest, then the whole state. It is then solved backward for implied extra votes. Denominator: Democratic governor votes ÷ total ballots cast. The method comes from the Cedar County Audit in Nebraska.

Step 1: Maricopa County (the largest county)

Step Value
Democratic share of ballots (S) 50.57% (2022: 790,352 of 1,562,758) → 56.55% (2026*: 881,998 of 1,559,706): +5.97 points
Democratic active-registration share (P) 30.05% (731,792 of 2,435,397) → 28.11% (727,925 of 2,589,827): −1.94 points
ΔG = ΔS − ΔP +7.92 points
Reverse step: ΔG − registration-share loss +5.97 (the residual)
Implied extra votes 881,998 − 50.57% × 1,559,706 = about +93,190
Registration-scaled gap (Nebraska "gap in votes", for comparison) 881,998 − 790,352 × (727,925 ÷ 731,792) = about +95,822

Maricopa is the largest county (4,689,558 people, 67.9% of the statewide implied extra votes). Its Democratic active registration fell by 3,867 while its active rolls grew by 154,430, so P fell 1.94 points; its S rose 5.97 points.
