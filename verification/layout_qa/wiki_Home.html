Arizona 2022 vs 2026* Governor Race: ΔG Method

Method developed by Morissa Benson (CD3 Vice Chair, Nebraska GOP). It began with her Cedar County Audit on the Nebraska Election Analysis wiki. That audit is where the ΔG method and the reverse step come from. This wiki applies the same arithmetic to all 15 Arizona counties. Arizona has party registration, so it uses the registration baseline, as the Nevada, North Carolina, Kansas and Pennsylvania analyses do. Data: Arizona Secretary of State results, official canvass and voter registration reports (azsos.gov, results.arizona.vote). Population comes from the U.S. Census Bureau. Compiled Nov 19, 2026. Code and data: Arizona-Election-Analysis.

County results in brief

  • Democratic active registration fell in 14 of 15 counties (1,270,544 → 1,239,489 statewide), while all active registration rose +230,898. Pinal is the exception: its Democratic active registration rose +465. P fell in all 15 counties, from −1.86 (Yavapai) to −6.55 (Santa Cruz).
  • S rose in all 15 counties, from +0.76 (Apache) to +8.89 (Yuma). Democratic governor votes rose in 13 counties and fell in 2 (Gila, Greenlee).
  • ΔG rose in all 15 counties. The ceiling is Yuma at +13.05; the lowest ΔG is Yavapai at +4.15. County median +7.03; statewide pooled +7.64.
  • Implied extra votes (votes above what the 2022 Democratic share of ballots would give on 2026* turnout) sum to +137,207 across the 15 counties; Maricopa alone is +93,190 (67.9%).
  • Locked Arizona formula: ΔG = 3.30 + 1.29 × (drop in Democratic registration share, points), R² 0.47, n = 15. See Locked Arizona Formula.
